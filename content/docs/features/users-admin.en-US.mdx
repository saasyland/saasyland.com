## Routes

`src/routes/admin.users.tsx` is a layout whose link tabs are `USER_TABS` in `custom/admin/users/user-tabs.tsx`, in the order they are shown, which `UserTabs` renders. A tab names its path and the full key of its label, its page's route sets the skeleton that `AdminUsersPending` draws below the tabs, and its label also names the page in the breadcrumbs. **All users** belongs to the auth area, **Customers** to the payments area (see [Orders and customers](/docs/features/orders#customers)) and **Invitations** to the team area, and each is labelled in its area's `navigation.<area>.json`, so a project without one of them has no tab for it. `/admin/users` redirects to `/admin/users/all`. Every tab takes its page title from `pages.admin.users`.

| Route                      | Content                                                                          |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `/admin/users/all`         | Every account in the data grid, searched and filtered on the server              |
| `/admin/users/customers`   | Every Polar customer with their account, orders and what they paid after refunds |
| `/admin/users/invitations` | The open invitations, with **Invite user**, **Resend** and **Revoke**            |
| `/admin/users/$userId`     | A user's profile: account details, sessions and the cards other areas add        |

## All users

```tsx
<DataGrid
  columns={columns}
  empty={t("empty")}
  id={USER_GRID_ID}
  label={t("label")}
  query={(request) => getUsersPageQuery(userZodSchemas.getUsers.parse(request))}
  rowActions={UserRowActions}
  search={t("search")}
  selectable
/>
```

- `getUsersPageQuery` calls the `getUsers` server function (`src/modules/user/use-cases/get-users.ts`, permission `user: ["list"]`). `userZodSchemas.getUsers` validates the grid's request, and paging, sorting, the search and every filter run on the server.
- The search matches the name and the email. The filters are the user as text, role and status as several choices, email verification and 2FA as yes or no, the time zone as several zones, and the created and updated dates as ranges of days.
- Status is not a column. `getUserStatus` (`user.utils.ts`) derives `banned`, `pending` or `active` from `banned` and `emailVerified`, and the server sorts and filters by the same SQL `case` expression. `UserStatusBadge` (`user-status-badge.tsx`) draws it in the table and on the profile.
- `useUserColumns` lives in `src/presentation/components/custom/admin/users/user-columns.tsx`; each name links to the user's profile, and the user column is pinned to the left. The user, role, status, email verification and created date show at first; 2FA, the time zone and the updated date wait in the Columns menu. The created and updated dates use the `common.dateTime` message, in the viewer's own time zone with its abbreviation, so both columns read and sort in one zone.
- Each row ends with `UserRowActions`, the grid's `rowActions`, pinned to the right: one ⋯ button (`RowMenu`) whose menu edits the user, changes the role, resets the password, signs in as the user, and, after a separator, bans and deletes. The CSV names the user as `name <email>` and writes the role, status and yes or no as the table shows them.

**Add user** opens `CreateUserDialog` (`create-user-dialog.tsx`), a TanStack Form validated by `userZodSchemas.createUser`: name, email, a starting password and a role. It calls `createUser` (`user: ["create"]`, and Better Auth also checks `user: ["set-role"]` for the role), then opens the new user's profile. Better Auth creates the account with its email unverified, so the first sign-in leads to `/auth/verify-email`, which sends the verification link.

## Profile

`src/routes/admin.users.$userId.tsx` loads the user, their sessions and the data of the other areas' cards at once and titles the page after the user; a user that does not exist is a not found page.

- `getUserQuery` reads the user's row (`get-user.ts`, `user: ["get"]`). `UserDetails` (`user-details.tsx`) lists the role, status, email verification, two-factor authentication, time zone and the created and updated dates, in the order of `ADMIN_USER_DETAILS` (`src/data/admin.ts`), which `AdminUserProfilePending` follows too.
- `listUserSessionsQuery` lists the user's live sessions, newest first, without their tokens (`list-user-sessions.ts`, `session: ["list"]`). `UserSessions` (`user-sessions.tsx`) shows each device and its activity, revokes one session with `revokeUserSession` and, after a confirmation, every session with `revokeUserSessions` (both `session: ["revoke"]`). `revokeUserSession` takes the session's ID and looks its token up on the server, as `revokeSession` does for the admin's own sessions.
- The cards below the account details are `USER_PROFILE_SECTIONS` in `custom/admin/users/user-profile-sections.tsx`, which `UserProfileSections` renders. A section names its card, its skeleton, which `AdminUserProfilePending` draws as well, and the data the page loads for the user. The auth area adds none. The licence area adds `UserLicense` (`user-license.tsx`), which reads `userLicenseQuery` (`get-user-license.ts`, `user: ["get"]`): the tier, the status, the purchase time, the consent to immediate delivery, the first activation, the first template download, the first paid lesson opened and, for an active licence, whether it can be refunded, which the server works out at the time of the request (see [Licences](/docs/features/licences#records-and-refunds)). Below it, `UserWithdrawals` (`user-withdrawals.tsx`) reads `userWithdrawalsQuery` (`get-user-withdrawals.ts`, `user: ["get"]`) and lists the account's withdrawal statements, newest first, with the name, email and order number given, how the buyer was identified, the refund terms when the statement arrived and whether the acknowledgement was emailed (see [Licences](/docs/features/licences#withdrawal-function)). Their copy is in `pages.admin.users.license`, which `src/routes/admin.users.tsx` names among its `NAMESPACES`.
- The licence card shows the tier's machine limit from `LICENSE_ACTIVATION_LIMITS` and, under **Machines in use**, the machines the key has activated in Polar: how many against Polar's limit, and each machine's label, system and activation time. `userActivationsQuery` (`get-user-activations.ts`, `user: ["get"]`) reads them with `polar.licenseKeys.get` for up to <RepositoryFact name="defaultPageSize" /> accounts at a time, each request with a timeout of <RepositoryFact name="licenseActivationsTimeoutSeconds" unit="second" />, and keeps the answer for <RepositoryFact name="licenseActivationsStaleMinutes" unit="minute" />. A licence without a key says that Polar has not issued one yet, and a key that Polar does not answer for keeps the rest of the card and says that Polar could not be reached.
- The first paid lesson opened shows the lesson and its course under its time. The licence shows the title with the courses area's `OpenedLessonName` and loads it with `lessonTitleQuery` (`get-lesson-title.ts`, `course: ["read"]`), so the licence module reads no course table. For a lesson since deleted, the row shows the time alone.
- The payments area adds `UserOrders`, the account's orders and what it paid after refunds per currency (see [Orders and customers](/docs/features/orders#customers)).

The header shows the user's name and email and the same actions menu as the table row.

## Actions

`UserActions` (`row-actions.tsx`) is the menu of a user, in the last column of the table (`UserRowActions`) and on the profile:

| Item               | Opens                    | Server function                                  |
| ------------------ | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **Edit user**      | `EditUserDialog`         | `updateUser` (`user: ["update"]`), the name only |
| **Change role**    | A submenu of the roles   | `setUserRole` (`user: ["set-role"]`)             |
| **Reset password** | `ResetPasswordDialog`    | `setUserPassword` (`user: ["set-password"]`)     |
| **Impersonate**    | Nothing, signs in        | `impersonateUser` (`user: ["impersonate"]`)      |
| **Ban user**       | `BanUserDialog`, confirm | `banUser` (`user: ["ban"]`)                      |
| **Unban user**     | Nothing                  | `unbanUser` (`user: ["ban"]`)                    |
| **Delete user**    | `DeleteUserDialog`       | `deleteUser` (`user: ["delete"]`)                |

- The signed-in admin's own row has no **Change role**, **Ban user** or **Delete user**, so an admin cannot lock themselves out; Better Auth refuses to ban or delete the caller anyway.
- **Impersonate** appears only for a user who is neither an admin nor banned: Better Auth refuses to impersonate an admin without `user: ["impersonate-admins"]`, which no role holds, and refuses a session to a banned user. See [Impersonation](/docs/features/impersonation).
- Deleting the user whose profile is open returns to `/admin/users/all`.
- Every success invalidates `USER_QUERY_KEYS.ALL`, or `SESSION_QUERY_KEYS.USER` for sessions, and shows a toast; errors, a missing permission among them, go through `useErrorMessage`. A ban also invalidates `SESSION_QUERY_KEYS.USER`, because it ends the user's sessions. The menu button shows that it is busy, and takes no press, while one of its actions runs.

Each server function checks its own permission with `authorized(...)` from `auth.middleware.ts`. The `user` and `session` statements are Better Auth's `defaultStatements`, and the `admin` role holds Better Auth's `adminAc`, so `APP_STATEMENTS` in `src/integrations/better-auth/auth.access.ts` adds only `admin` and `settings` for the console. The `/admin` guard admits only roles that hold `admin: ["access"]`.

## Invitations

**Invite user** on `/admin/users/invitations` opens `InviteUserDialog` (`invite-user-dialog.tsx`), a TanStack Form validated by `invitationZodSchemas.createInvitation`: the email address, a role from `ROLE_VALUES` in `auth.roles.ts` and the language of the email, the admin's own language to begin with. `createInvitation` (`src/modules/invitation/use-cases/create-invitation.ts`, permission `user: ["create"]`, rate limited by `INVITATION_RATE_LIMIT`) refuses an address that already has an account or an open invitation with `CONFLICT`, stores the invitation and emails the link with `sendInvitationEmail`. When the email cannot be sent, the invitation is removed again.

The `invitation` table keeps who invited whom and what happened to it:

| Column                       | Holds                                                                                       |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `email`, `role`, `locale`    | The invited address, in lower case, the role the account gets and the language of the email |
| `token_hash`                 | The SHA-256 hash of the link's token; the token itself is only in the email                 |
| `invited_by`, `created_at`   | The admin who invited, and when                                                             |
| `sent_at`, `expires_at`      | When the last link was sent, and when it stops working: `INVITATION_LIFETIME_DAYS` later    |
| `accepted_by`, `accepted_at` | The account that accepted it, and when                                                      |
| `revoked_by`, `revoked_at`   | The admin who revoked it, and when                                                          |

The user foreign keys are `ON DELETE SET NULL`, so the record outlives the accounts it names. A partial unique index on `email` where `accepted_at` and `revoked_at` are empty allows one open invitation per address.

The tab lists the open invitations with `listInvitations` (`user: ["list"]`), pending or with an expired link, in the `DataGrid` with `useInvitationColumns` (`invitation-columns.tsx`), which searches the email and the inviter and filters by email, role, status, inviter and the sent and expiry days in the browser; each row starts with a checkbox, for exporting the selected invitations, and `InvitationRowActions` ends it with its ⋯ menu holding **Resend** and, after a separator, **Revoke**. **Resend** (`resendInvitation`, rate limited like the invitation) sends a new link, which replaces the token and restarts the lifetime, so the earlier link stops working. **Revoke** (`revokeInvitation`) asks first, then records the admin and the time; the link then says that the invitation was withdrawn.

The link opens `/auth/invite?token=…` (`src/routes/auth.invite.tsx`). `getInvitation` returns the invited address, the role and the status of the link (`INVITATION_STATUS`), and the page shows one of these:

- **Pending, signed out:** the address, a name and a password, which `acceptInvitation` (rate limited as a token) uses to create the account with Better Auth's `createUser`, with the role and the address already verified, before it signs the visitor in and opens `/admin` or `/app` by role. The password passes the same policy and Have I Been Pwned check as an admin's new user. **Continue with Google** or **GitHub** comes back to the same page signed in.
- **Pending, signed in with the invited address:** **Accept invitation**, which `claimInvitation` (signed in, rate limited as a token) answers by giving the account the role and a verified address.
- **Pending, signed in with another address:** both addresses and **Sign out**.
- **Expired, revoked, accepted or unknown:** what happened and a way to sign in.

Each acceptance marks the invitation accepted only while it is open and its link has not expired, so one link opens one account. The roles themselves are `admin` and `customer`, set later per user with **Change role**.
