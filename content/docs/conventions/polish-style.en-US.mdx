Polish is the language of the person who builds SaaSy Land, and its readers are Polish engineers and Polish buyers who notice a sentence translated word for word at once. Every Polish message in `messages/pl-PL/`, docs page, blog post and changelog entry follows this page. The glossary at the end is the single source of the Polish terms: `POLISH_TERMS`, `POLISH_NAMES` and `POLISH_PHRASES` in `src/data/polish-glossary.ts`, which this page prints. A term that the glossary does not decide yet is added there first, in the same pull request that uses it.

## Write the meaning, not the sentence

Read the English, close it and write what a Polish senior engineer would write to say the same thing to a colleague or a customer. Keep the facts, the numbers, the placeholders and the tags; change everything else that makes the sentence sound translated.

- Polish puts the verb to work where English stacks nouns: `Generowanie projektów pojawi się w kolejnym wydaniu`, not `Funkcjonalność generowania projektów zostanie dostarczona`.
- The verb already says who acts, so the pronoun usually goes: `Skopiuj klucz licencyjny`, not `Skopiuj swój klucz licencyjny`. `Twój` stays where ownership is the point, as in `Twoja baza danych, nie cudza`.
- An English punchline that rests on a pun or an idiom gets a Polish sentence that makes the same point, not the same words: `You just shouldn’t have to` is `Tylko po co?`, not `Po prostu nie powinieneś musieć`.
- A slogan, a heading and a button stay short. Polish words are longer than English ones, so cut words rather than meaning.

## Form of address and register

The site speaks to one reader in the second person singular, capitalised as a courtesy: `Ty`, `Twój`, `Ciebie`, `Ci`, `Tobie`. Polish spelling capitalises these pronouns in letters and in text addressed to one person, and the account pages, the checkout and the emails are exactly that, so the landing page and the docs use the same form instead of a second rule. [One form of address](/docs/conventions/translations#one-form-of-address) explains the check that fails `Pan`, `Pani`, `Państwo` and the lowercase pronouns.

- The site does not know the reader’s gender, so it never addresses the reader in the past tense or with an adjective that shows one: `Za co już zapłacono`, `Po zakupie`, `Jeśli masz już licencję`, not `za co już zapłaciłeś` or `jesteś gotowy`.
- Buttons and links are imperatives (`Zapisz`, `Kup pakiet Komplet`, `Przejdź do płatności`); headings, menu entries and table headers are nouns (`Ustawienia`, `Licencja`, `Faktury`); confirmations are impersonal (`Zapisano`, `Skopiowano`, `Miejsce aktywacji zwolnione`).
- An error says what failed and what to do next, without an apology: `Nie udało się zapisać zmian. Spróbuj ponownie.`
- The tone is direct, confident and precise. No exclamation marks, no superlatives the facts do not carry, and no sales phrases that only exist in English marketing.

## No calques and no half-translated English

A calque copies the English words and loses the Polish meaning: `produkcyjny starter` reads as a starter that manufactures something, and `hydraulika` is pipes in a bathroom. The phrase table below lists the ones that reached the site and what to write instead.

A sentence is either Polish or code. English appears in Polish text only as a product name, a code identifier, a file path, a command, or a term of art from the glossary that Polish developers use in English, such as `stack`, `framework`, `commit`, `pull request` and `webhook`. Such a term is written without italics or quotation marks and takes Polish endings the way developers say it: `commita`, `w pull requeście`, `frameworku`, `stacku`. Everything else is translated, including the words that tempt a half-translation: a release is `wydanie`, a deploy is `wdrożenie`, a merge is `scalenie`, a feature is `funkcja`.

## Typography

- Quotation marks are „…”, with »…« inside them. Code and identifiers keep their own quotes inside inline code.
- A dash between clauses is a dash with a space on each side (`–` or `—`), never a hyphen. A range takes an en dash without spaces: `10–20`.
- Headings, titles, buttons and menu entries capitalise only the first word and proper names: `Zasady zwrotów`, not `Zasady Zwrotów`. Months, weekdays and language names are lowercase: `po angielsku`, `w poniedziałek`.
- Polish needs a comma before every subordinate clause (`że`, `który`, `gdy`, `jeśli`, `aby`, `ponieważ`, `co`) and takes no comma before `i`, `oraz` or `lub` in a list.
- Numbers, dates, currencies and units come from the formatter, which writes a non-breaking space between thousands and a decimal comma, so they are never typed. The site does not bind single-letter words such as `w` and `z` to the next word, so the copy does not type non-breaking spaces by hand either.

## The glossary check [#glossary-check]

`bun run check` runs `check:glossary`, which fails whenever Polish copy uses a term from a never column below: the messages in `messages/pl-PL/`, the emails among them, every Polish docs page, blog post and changelog entry with its title, description and questions, the navigation titles in `meta.pl-PL.json` and the Polish text of `content/courses/saasy-land-courses.json`. It prints the file, the line, the word it found and the term the glossary prefers.

- A term fails in each of its inflected forms. In `src/data/polish-glossary.ts` a bar marks where the ending of a word starts, so `wspier|ać` fails `wspierane`, `wspieranej` and `wspieramy` but not `wsparcie`; the tables below print the term without the bar. A word without a bar fails only as written, so `admin` does not fail `administracyjny`.
- Code, links, paths, quotations in „…” and an English term given in parentheses after `ang.` are not Polish prose, so the check skips them.
- A capitalised word fails only where a sentence, a heading, a list item, a table cell or a link text starts, so names such as Better Auth and Stripe Billing do not fail `auth` and `billing`.
- A term that the glossary also prefers for another meaning, such as `karta`, which is right for a browser tab and wrong for a tab on a page, depends on its sentence, so the check leaves it to the reader: <RepositoryFact name="polishContextTerms" />.

### Product and tier names
