## Do EU consumers have a right of withdrawal for digital products?

Yes, by default. The Consumer Rights Directive, 2011/83/EU, gives a consumer who concludes a contract at a distance, which includes every online purchase, 14 days to withdraw without giving a reason (Article 9(1)). For digital content that is not supplied on a tangible medium, the 14 days run from the day the contract is concluded (Article 9(2)(c)).

Two definitions decide whether this applies to you:

- **Digital content** is "data which are produced and supplied in digital form". The Consumer Rights Directive takes that definition from the Digital Content Directive, 2019/770 (Article 2(1)). A downloadable program, a set of templates or an e-book is digital content.
- **A consumer** is a natural person acting for purposes outside their trade, business, craft or profession (Article 2(1) of the Consumer Rights Directive). A company buying your product has no right of withdrawal under the directive, though national law can extend it, as the Polish example below shows.

If you sell to consumers, you therefore start with a 14-day window in which the buyer can change their mind. For a physical product that is manageable: the goods come back. For digital content it is not, because what you supplied cannot be returned. The law has an answer for that, and it is precise.

## When the right of withdrawal ends for digital content

Article 16(m) of the Consumer Rights Directive, as amended by the Omnibus Directive, 2019/2161, excludes the right of withdrawal for digital content not supplied on a tangible medium "if the performance has begun" and, where the consumer has to pay, all three of these are true:

1. **Prior express consent.** "The consumer has provided prior express consent to begin the performance during the right of withdrawal period."
2. **Acknowledgement.** "The consumer has provided acknowledgement that he thereby loses his right of withdrawal."
3. **Confirmation.** "The trader has provided confirmation in accordance with Article 7(2) or Article 8(7)."

Each word in the first two matters. _Prior_ means before supply begins, so at checkout. _Express_ means the consumer states it themselves, and an unticked checkbox that the buyer must tick is the usual way to ask. The acknowledgement is a separate statement from the request: asking for immediate supply is not the same as knowing what it costs.

German courts read _express_ strictly. In 2019 the Regional Court of Cologne prohibited a digital store's purchase flow in which clicking the buy button also gave the consent to immediate performance and the acknowledgement: the consumer had to act for them separately, and the court cited the Commission's guidance that a box ticked in advance is not enough ([LG Köln, 21 May 2019, 31 O 372/17](https://nrwe.justiz.nrw.de/lgs/koeln/lg_koeln/j2019/31_O_372_17_Urteil_20190521.html)).

The exception also needs the performance to _have begun_. The Digital Content Directive says when supply happens: the trader has complied with the obligation to supply when "the digital content or any means suitable for accessing or downloading the digital content is made available or accessible to the consumer" (Article 5(2)(a)). saasyland.com treats the issue of the licence key as that moment, because the key is the means of access. Decide the moment for your own product, write it down in your terms, and make your system record it.

## The confirmation on a durable medium

Article 8(7) requires the trader to confirm a distance contract on a durable medium within a reasonable time after it is concluded. The confirmation must include the pre-contractual information of Article 6(1), unless the consumer already has it on a durable medium, and, "where applicable, the confirmation of the consumer's prior express consent and acknowledgment in accordance with point (m) of Article 16".

A durable medium is any instrument that lets the consumer store information addressed to them personally, keep it accessible for an adequate period and reproduce it unchanged (Article 2(10)). The directive's own model withdrawal text, in Annex I, gives email as an example of one. A page on your site is a weak substitute: you can change it, and the buyer may never save it. An email the buyer keeps is the safer choice.

## What happens when a step is missing

If any of the three conditions is missing, Article 16(m) does not apply and the consumer keeps the full 14 days. Article 14(4)(b) then adds the expensive part: the consumer "shall bear no cost" for digital content supplied, in full or in part, where

- they did not give prior express consent to the performance beginning before the end of the 14 days,
- they did not acknowledge that they lose the right of withdrawal when giving that consent, or
- the trader failed to provide the confirmation required by Article 8(7).

In practice, a buyer can download everything, use it for most of the 14 days and still get the whole price back, with nothing deducted for what they used. The step most easily missed is the email: the checkbox is visible in the checkout, while the confirmation is easy to treat as a courtesy, and it is the third condition.

## The withdrawal button since 19 June 2026

There is one more rule for online sellers. Article 11a of the directive, inserted by Directive 2023/2673 and applied by member states from 19 June 2026, requires a trader that concludes distance contracts through an online interface to offer a **withdrawal function**: an easy-to-find, clearly labelled function, worded "withdraw from contract here" or an unambiguous equivalent, available throughout the withdrawal period, through which the consumer can send their withdrawal online. It applies wherever the right of withdrawal still exists, for instance when the consent was never given.

## The same rules in Polish law

The directive binds member states, and each one writes it into its own law, in its own language and its own legal terms. The right of withdrawal is the "Widerrufsrecht" in Germany, the "droit de rétractation" in France and the "prawo odstąpienia od umowy" in Poland. Your pages should use the terms of the law that applies to your buyers, in their language.

Poland's Consumer Rights Act of 30 May 2014, in the consolidated text published in 2026, implements Article 16(m) in art. 38 ust. 1 pkt 13. The right of withdrawal does not apply to contracts:

> o dostarczanie treści cyfrowych niedostarczanych na nośniku materialnym, za które konsument jest zobowiązany do zapłaty ceny, jeżeli przedsiębiorca rozpoczął świadczenie za wyraźną i uprzednią zgodą konsumenta, który został poinformowany przed rozpoczęciem świadczenia, że po spełnieniu świadczenia przez przedsiębiorcę utraci prawo odstąpienia od umowy, i przyjął to do wiadomości, a przedsiębiorca przekazał konsumentowi potwierdzenie, o którym mowa w art. 15 ust. 1 i 2 albo art. 21 ust. 1

In English: for the supply of digital content not on a tangible medium that the consumer pays for, if the trader began the performance with the consumer's express and prior consent, the consumer was told before it began that they would lose the right of withdrawal and acknowledged it, and the trader sent the confirmation required by art. 15 or art. 21. Art. 21 ust. 1 requires that confirmation on a durable medium, including the information about the consent, and art. 36 pkt 2 repeats Article 14(4)(b): the consumer bears no cost when any of the steps is missing.

Polish law also shows how a national text can go beyond the directive. Under art. 7aa, the consumer rules on withdrawal also apply to a sole trader who concludes a contract directly related to their business, when the contract is not of a professional nature for them, judged by the business activities they have registered. A Polish shop owner buying software for their shop can therefore have the right of withdrawal, while a developer buying a developer tool most likely does not.

## How saasyland.com implements it

saasyland.com sells licences for the SaaSy Land CLI through Polar, a merchant of record. Polar's buyer terms make Polar the seller of the purchase, while SaaSy Land licenses the product and supplies it: supply begins when the licence key is issued, right after payment. The flow below collects, records and confirms the consent whichever of the two counts as the trader, and every step is in the [public source](https://github.com/saasyland/saasyland.com) under the MIT licence.

### 1. One consent, on Polar's checkout

Polar's checkout takes custom fields, and each licence product requires a checkbox field with the slug <RepositoryFact name="licenseWithdrawalConsentField" />. Its label states the request and the acknowledgement: "I ask SaaSy Land to supply my purchase immediately, and I acknowledge that I lose my <RepositoryFact name="refundWithdrawalDays" />-day right of withdrawal once supply begins, when my licence key is issued." [`start-checkout.ts`](https://github.com/saasyland/saasyland.com/blob/main/src/modules/license/use-cases/start-checkout.ts) sends no value for the field, so the box starts empty and only the buyer can tick it. When the buyer pays, Polar checks the fields against the product and accepts only a ticked box for a required checkbox, so no payment goes through without the consent, and Polar stores the ticked box on the order. `polar:verify` fails while any licence product does not require the field.

The consent is asked once, on the page where the buyer pays. A box the buyer ticks there, right before paying, is the plainest record of express consent, and no extra step stands between the buyer and the payment.

### 2. The same terms beforehand, in the buyer's language

A custom field has one label, so the box on Polar's page is in English. The site says it first, in whichever of its <RepositoryFact name="languages" /> languages the buyer is reading: under the buy buttons, a notice says that Polar is the merchant of record and asks for the consent on the payment page, that ticking the box ends the right of withdrawal once the licence key is issued, and until when a full refund remains possible. The buy buttons then open Polar's checkout straight away, in the buyer's language where Polar translates its checkout, and the notice says so when the payment page will open in English. The notice is `CheckoutNotice` in [`checkout.tsx`](https://github.com/saasyland/saasyland.com/blob/main/src/presentation/components/custom/app/checkout.tsx).

### 3. A confirmation email, the durable medium

When Polar reports the payment with `order.paid`, the webhook grants the licence and sends the purchase confirmation by email, in the language the buyer checked out in. It names the order, its date, the tier and the amount paid, and then:

- with the consent recorded, it repeats that the buyer asked for immediate supply and acknowledged losing the right of withdrawal, so the right does not apply to this order;
- without it, it says the buyer can withdraw within <RepositoryFact name="refundWithdrawalDays" unit="day" /> of the order date and how;
- either way, it gives the date until which a full refund is still possible and links the Terms of Service, the Commercial Licence and the Refund Policy in force on the day of the order.

The email's template is [`purchase-confirmation-email.tsx`](https://github.com/saasyland/saasyland.com/blob/main/src/presentation/emails/purchase-confirmation-email.tsx).

### 4. Delivery records

The licence keeps four timestamps, each empty until it happens:

| Record                  | When it is written                                                             |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `withdrawal_consent_at` | When the paid order carries the consent; the time is the order's creation time |
| `activated_at`          | The first time the key is activated on any machine                             |
| `delivered_at`          | The first time the CLI downloads templates with the key                        |
| `lesson_opened_at`      | The first time the licence opens a paid lesson, with the lesson's ID           |

The webhook validates the consent field with Zod before it writes the first record, the [CLI licence API](/docs/features/cli-licence-api) writes `activated_at` and `delivered_at`, and the course pages write `lesson_opened_at` when they serve the first paid lesson. A new purchase on the same account resets all four, so they always describe the current order. These records decide refunds, and the [Refund Policy](/refunds) says that they may be shared with Polar to answer a refund request or a payment dispute.

### 5. A refund policy more generous than the law

The consent ends the statutory right, but saasyland.com still refunds in full within <RepositoryFact name="refundWithdrawalDays" unit="day" /> of the purchase as long as the purchase has not been used: the CLI has not downloaded templates with the key, and no paid lesson has been opened. Signing in, viewing the key and activating it on a machine do not count as use. After first use, the site fixes or refunds what is missing, defective or not as described, where the law entitles the buyer to that remedy.

[`license.refunds.ts`](https://github.com/saasyland/saasyland.com/blob/main/src/modules/license/license.refunds.ts) turns the records into one of four answers, which an admin sees on the buyer's profile:

| Answer                | When                                                                                                            |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `refundable`          | Within the period, the buyer consented, the CLI has not downloaded templates and no paid lesson has been opened |
| `statutoryWithdrawal` | Within the period and no consent was recorded, so the statutory right applies                                   |
| `used`                | Within the period, the buyer consented and the CLI has downloaded templates or a paid lesson has been opened    |
| `expired`             | The period has ended                                                                                            |

A refund window as long as the withdrawal period makes the checkbox an easier thing to tick: the buyer gives up the statutory right, and keeps a full refund until first use for the same number of days.

### 6. A licence that ends with a refund or chargeback

The [Commercial Licence](/licence) says that a full refund, a chargeback or another reversal of the payment ends the licence for that purchase entirely: the key is revoked and every right granted under the licence ends, including the rights to applications generated with it. Rights under the public code's MIT licence and mandatory consumer remedies are unaffected.

The code follows the text. When Polar reports a fully refunded order, the webhook marks the licence `refunded`; a partial refund keeps it. When Polar revokes the licence-key benefit, the licence is marked `revoked`. The CLI licence API refuses a key in either state, and the order is recorded so that a late webhook cannot grant it again. See [Licences](/docs/features/licences#webhooks-and-licences).

### 7. Affiliate commission held longer than the refund window

The [affiliate terms](/affiliates) keep each commission pending for <RepositoryFact name="affiliateHoldDays" unit="day" />, well past the <RepositoryFact name="refundWithdrawalDays" unit="day" /> refund window, so that refunds and most payment disputes settle before anything is paid. A refund or chargeback during the hold cancels or reduces the commission; one after payout is deducted from the next. On 17 November 2026 the programme has not opened yet, but its terms are already published.

### 8. A withdrawal function

The footer of every page links **Withdraw from contract here** to [`/withdrawal`](/withdrawal), and the licence page of the buyer's account opens the same statement in a dialog. The buyer gives or confirms their name, the email address for the acknowledgement and the order number, which are filled in when they are signed in, and sends the statement with **Confirm withdrawal**. The form takes a statement whether or not the right still applies; that is decided afterwards. [`record-withdrawal.ts`](https://github.com/saasyland/saasyland.com/blob/main/src/modules/withdrawal/use-cases/record-withdrawal.ts) stores it with the refund terms of the matched licence at that moment, emails the buyer an acknowledgement of receipt with the statement and the date and time of its submission, and notifies the site, and an admin sees the statements on the buyer's profile. Nothing is refunded automatically: the refund is made in Polar. The whole function is in [`src/modules/withdrawal/`](https://github.com/saasyland/saasyland.com/tree/main/src/modules/withdrawal).

## A checklist for your own checkout

1. Decide when supply begins for your product, and write it into your terms.
2. Before payment, ask for the consent and the acknowledgement with a required checkbox that starts empty, in the buyer's language.
3. Make the payment impossible without the consent, enforced where the payment is taken, not only on the page.
4. Record the consent with the order, and record the moment of supply and of first use.
5. Send a confirmation email that includes the consent and the acknowledgement, and the order's details.
6. When the consent is missing, keep the full 14 days, and remember that the buyer then bears no cost.
7. Offer the withdrawal function wherever a right of withdrawal remains.
8. Make the refund policy, the licence and any affiliate hold say the same thing about refunds.
9. Have a lawyer review the result.
