title: "Search and social previews"
description: "Page metadata, structured data, robots.txt, the sitemaps and the social cards the build renders for every prerendered page."
---

Every page describes itself to search engines and link previews through its route's `head`. The build turns those descriptions into the sitemap of the prerendered pages and a social card for each of them, so both are static files in `dist/client` and nothing about them runs in the Worker. The course pages are read from D1 for each request, so the Worker lists them in a sitemap of its own (see [Course sitemap](#course-sitemap)).

## Page metadata

`buildPageHead` in `src/lib/seo.ts` builds the head of every page. Most routes call it through `pageHead(ROUTES.X)`; docs pages, blog posts and course pages call it directly. It emits:

- the title, with the locale's separator and `APP_NAME`, the description, the canonical URL and an `alternate` link with `hreflang` for every locale and for `x-default`;
- for a page of a sequence, `rel="prev"` and `rel="next"` links to its neighbours (`sequence`): each page of the blog's archive names its own address as canonical, never the first page's, and links the pages before and after it;
- Open Graph: `og:site_name`, `og:title` (the page title without the site name, as Open Graph expects), `og:description`, `og:type`, `og:url`, `og:locale` and, for a page that belongs to a section, `article:section`;
- for a blog post, `article:published_time` and `article:modified_time`, from the `date` and `updated` of its frontmatter, and, from its `author`, the `author` meta tag with the author's name and `article:author` with the URL of the author's page in the page's locale;
- the social image: `og:image`, `og:image:type`, `og:image:width` and `og:image:height` (<RepositoryFact name="socialImageWidth" /> by <RepositoryFact name="socialImageHeight" /> pixels) and `og:image:alt`, with `twitter:card` set to `summary_large_image`, `twitter:title`, `twitter:description`, `twitter:image` and `twitter:image:alt`.

A route's head keeps one tag per name or property, so a list such as the `article:tag` of a blog post's tags is rendered by the post page itself, and React places those tags in the document head. For the same reason the root route adds `og:locale:alternate` for the other locales in the document itself. The root route also sets the `robots` directive: `ROBOTS.FULL_PREVIEWS` in a `production` build, which lets search engines show large image previews and full snippets, and `ROBOTS.NON_PRODUCTION` (`noindex, nofollow`) in every other build mode, which keeps preview and test deployments out of search results.

A page keeps itself out of search results with the `robots` meta tag `ROBOTS.NOINDEX` (`noindex`), which `buildPageHead` writes when the route passes it as `robots`. The course catalogue does while no course is published, and so does every lesson that a signed-out visitor may not watch: one that needs an account or a licence, and one being recorded (`isOpenToVisitors` in `src/modules/course/course.utils.ts`). Every not-found page does too: `notFoundHead` in `src/lib/not-found.ts` gives it the localized title and description from `errors.not-found` and `noindex`, without a canonical link or social card.

Docs pages name the section their breadcrumb shows: `fumadocs.indexes.ts` reads it with Fumadocs' `getBreadcrumbItems` when it builds the docs index, so `/docs` and the first page of each section have none. Blog posts name the blog, with the title from `pages.blog`.

## Structured data

`src/lib/seo.ts` also writes the JSON-LD that describes the site to search engines, and `src/lib/blog-seo.ts` the JSON-LD of the blog's pages. Every JSON-LD script is serialized with `<` escaped, so no value can close the `script` element.

- The home page describes the organisation with `ORGANIZATION_STRUCTURED_DATA_HTML`: an `Organization` with `APP_NAME`, `APP_URL`, `CONTACT_EMAIL`, its logo at `ORGANIZATION_LOGO.PATH` and, in `sameAs`, its profiles on other sites, `APP_GITHUB_OWNER_URL` and `APP_INSTAGRAM_URL`. `sameAs` names the GitHub organisation rather than the repository, because it lists profiles of the organisation itself. Google reads this from the home page, so other pages do not repeat it.
- A blog post adds a `BlogPosting`, a `BreadcrumbList` and, when its frontmatter has `faq` entries, an `FAQPage` (`buildBlogPostStructuredDataHtml`); see [Blog](/docs/features/blog). The `BlogPosting` gives the headline, the description, the cover as an `ImageObject` of the `BLOG_COVER` size, the dates, the language as `inLanguage`, the tags in that language as `keywords`, the author as a reference to a `Person` node of the same graph, and the organisation, logo included, as `publisher`. The `Person` comes from `BLOG_AUTHORS` and the post's messages: the name, the role as `jobTitle`, the photo as `image`, the author's page in the page's locale as `url` and, with `#person` appended, as `@id`, and the author's profiles as `sameAs`. The breadcrumb leads from `APP_NAME` through the blog to the post, with the URLs and the blog's title of the page's locale.
- An author's page, `/authors/<id>`, adds a `ProfilePage` with its name, description and language whose `mainEntity` is the same `Person` (`buildAuthorStructuredDataHtml`), so every post's `author` and the profile name one node; see [Blog](/docs/features/blog#authors).
- The blog index adds a `Blog` with its name, description, language and publisher, listing every published post of its locale as a `BlogPosting` with its headline, URL and publication date, and the posts of its first page as an `ItemList` (`buildBlogStructuredDataHtml`).
- Each later page of the blog's archive, `/blog/page/<n>`, adds a `CollectionPage` that is part of the `Blog`, its posts as an `ItemList` numbered across the whole archive, and a `BreadcrumbList` from `APP_NAME` through the blog to the page (`buildBlogArchiveStructuredDataHtml`); see [Blog](/docs/features/blog#routes).
- The landing page's pricing section describes each tier as a `Product` (`TierStructuredData` in `src/presentation/components/custom/pricing.tsx`): `APP_NAME` and the tier's name, its tagline as the description, `APP_NAME` as its `Brand`, the site card as its image, and an `Offer` with the tier's price from `TIER_PRICES`, the currency of `CURRENCY_FORMAT` that the page prints, `InStock` availability, the pricing section's address and the organisation as the seller. A licence for downloadable code could also be a `SoftwareApplication`, but Google shows a software app's rich result only with ratings or reviews, which the site does not collect, while a `Product` with an `Offer` qualifies for a product snippet with its price. The regional prices are not in it: the Worker writes them into the page for each visitor, while the regular price is the one every visitor's page shows first.
- The landing page's FAQ adds no `FAQPage`: Google stopped showing FAQ rich results in May 2026, and every answer is in the prerendered HTML already, inside the accordion's collapsed panels, where search engines read it.
- A course page adds a `Course` (`courseStructuredDataHtml` in `src/modules/course/course.utils.ts`): the title, the summary, the address and language of the page, the level as the page shows it, the cover as `image`, the outcomes as `teaches`, the prerequisites as `coursePrerequisites` and the organisation as `provider`. Its `offers` hold an `Offer` for each course access in `COURSE_ACCESS_GRANTS` at its price, in the same currency, or a single free one at 0 when no lesson needs a licence; their `category` is `Free`, `Partially Free` or `Paid` from the lessons' access levels, and `isAccessibleForFree` is true for a free course. `hasCourseInstance` is an `Online` `CourseInstance` whose `courseWorkload` is the length of the recorded video, rounded up to the minute, once a lesson has one.
- The course catalogue adds an `ItemList` of the published courses' addresses in its language (`catalogueStructuredDataHtml`), the summary page from which Google reads a list of courses.

## Social cards

Each prerendered page has its own card at `/images/og/<canonical path>.png`, and the home page at `/images/og/index.png` (`socialImagePath`). The card shows the wordmark, the section in the accent colour when there is one, the page's `og:title` and the domain, over the grid and glow of the site's backgrounds. Its alt text is that text in the page's locale: the title, the section and `APP_NAME`, joined by the locale's title separator.

Pages that the Worker renders for each request, <RepositoryFact name="requestRenderedRoutes" /> and every path below them, are not prerendered and share `/images/og/site.png`, which shows the wordmark alone and has `APP_NAME` as its alt text. `REQUEST_RENDERED_ROUTES` in `src/routes.ts` lists them beside the other paths: `shouldPrerender` (`src/lib/prerender.ts`) leaves them out of prerendering and `buildPageHead` gives them the site card unless the route passes an image of its own. A course's pages do: `courseShareImage` (`src/modules/course/course.utils.ts`) gives the course page and each of its lessons the course's cover as `og:image` and `twitter:image`, with the course title as its alt text, so every course has a card of its own; see [Course covers](/docs/features/courses#course-covers). A course without a cover shares the site card. The build does not draw cards for courses, because Takumi runs in Node during the build and the courses are rows in D1 that an admin changes after the deploy.

The cards are React components, `PageCard` and `SiteCard` in `src/presentation/social-card.tsx`. `LogoCard` in the same file draws the organisation's logo, the mark of `APP_LOGO_PATH` on the light background, as a square PNG at `ORGANIZATION_LOGO.PATH`, the image the structured data names as the logo. `createSocialCardRenderer` in `src/integrations/takumi/takumi.render.tsx` draws them with [Takumi](https://takumi.kane.tw/docs/) (`@takumi-rs/core`), a Rust renderer with native Node bindings that needs no browser:

- The colours are the light theme's tokens. The renderer reads `src/presentation/styles/globals.css`, so `var(--background)`, `var(--foreground)`, `var(--ring)` and the `field-grid` and `field-signal` classes are the site's own.
- The fonts are the files the site serves. `takumi.fonts.ts` reads every `@font-face` rule with a `url()` source from `fonts.css`, the Geist and Geist Mono subsets with their unicode ranges, and from `@fontsource-variable/noto-sans-jp`, because the site leaves Japanese to the system font. Takumi loads only the subsets a card's text uses. Each face is registered under `APP_NAME` followed by its family name: Takumi bundles its own Latin-only Geist, which would otherwise be chosen instead of the site's Cyrillic and Latin Extended subsets.
- The logo is `APP_LOGO_PATH` from `src/presentation/branding`, the path `Wordmark` draws.

To change the design, edit `social-card.tsx`, run `bun run build:test` and open the cards in `dist/client/images/og`.

## Sitemap and robots.txt

`public/robots.txt` allows every path and names `https://saasyland.com/sitemap.xml`; `robots.test.ts` fails when that line does not match `APP_URL` and `SITEMAP_PATH`. `sitemap.xml` is a sitemap index: it names `sitemap-pages.xml`, the sitemap of the prerendered pages, and every sitemap a feature serves on its own, today `/sitemap-courses.xml`, so search engines find all of them from the one address `robots.txt` gives.

`scripts/generate-seo-files.ts` runs at the end of every build script, after `optimize-prerendered-html.ts`. It reads the head of every prerendered page in `dist/client` with `readPageHead` from `src/lib/page-head.ts`, then:

1. Writes `dist/client/sitemap-pages.xml` (`PAGES_SITEMAP_PATH`) with every page whose `robots` directive allows indexing and whose path `robots.txt` does not disallow (`listedPages` and `buildSitemap` in `src/lib/sitemap.ts`, with the RFC 9309 matching of `src/lib/robots.ts`). Each `<url>` lists the page's own `alternate` links as `xhtml:link` entries, `x-default` included. The build fails when a page names an alternate that the sitemap does not list, or when a URL appears twice. `ROBOTS.NON_PRODUCTION` does not count as a page's own directive, so every build mode writes the same sitemap.
2. Writes `dist/client/sitemap.xml`, the index of the sitemaps in `SITEMAPS`: `PAGES_SITEMAP_PATH` and `ROUTES.COURSES_SITEMAP` (`buildSitemapIndex`). A site built without the `courses` feature (see [Tests follow the features](/docs/conventions/testing#tests-follow-the-features)) leaves `ROUTES.COURSES_SITEMAP` out of the list, and its index names the pages' sitemap alone.
3. Writes the RSS feed of the blog in every locale from the heads of the blog index and the posts the sitemap lists (`buildBlogFeeds` in `src/lib/feed.ts`); see [Blog](/docs/features/blog#feed).
4. Renders the site card and every page's card into `dist/client/images/og`, and the organisation's logo, and fails when two pages name the same image.

It writes the sitemaps and the feeds as one list of XML files, so a site built without the `content` feature, which holds the blog, writes the sitemaps alone, and prints the files it wrote, how many pages the sitemap lists, how many cards it wrote and how long it took. A blog post's `<url>` carries a `<lastmod>`: `readPageHead` reads the post's `article:modified_time`, and `buildSitemap` writes its day as `YYYY-MM-DD`, in every locale. No other prerendered page has one, because docs, landing and legal pages carry no reliable modification date, and the time of the build would claim changes that never happened.

Takumi and the script run only at build time, and `src/server.ts` serves `sitemap.xml`, `sitemap-pages.xml` and the cards from static assets like any other file, with the cache policy `public/_headers` sets for `/images/*`.

### Course sitemap

The course pages are read from D1 for each request and are not prerendered, so the build cannot list them. The Worker answers `/sitemap-courses.xml` (`ROUTES.COURSES_SITEMAP`) itself: `src/server.ts` hands the path to `serveCourseSitemap` in `src/modules/course/course.delivery.ts`, which reads D1 at the time of the request and lists, each in every locale with the same `hreflang` alternates as the page (`localizedSitemapPages` in `src/lib/sitemap.ts`):

- `/courses`, once a course is published;
- every published course;
- every published lesson that a signed-out visitor may watch (`isOpenToVisitors`): a free preview whose recording is published, or a free text lesson. A lesson that needs an account or a licence, and a lesson being recorded, are left out and carry `noindex`.

Each `<lastmod>` is the day of the latest `updatedAt` stored for what the page shows: for a lesson, its row and its video's; for a course, its row, its chapters and its published lessons with their videos; for the catalogue, the latest course. An admin's change therefore reaches the sitemap with the next request. The response is `application/xml` with `Cache-Control: public, max-age=3600`, and `HEAD` gets the headers alone. While no course is published the sitemap is empty, and `/courses` carries `noindex`.

## Tests

- `seo.test.ts`, `page-head.test.tsx`, `sitemap.test.ts`, `feed.test.ts` and `robots.test.ts` in `src/lib/__test__` cover the tags and the structured data, reading the tags back, the sitemaps and their index, the feeds and the robots rules. `course.utils.test.ts` covers the `Course` and `ItemList` structured data, `course.delivery.test.ts` the course sitemap with its dates, and `marketing-sections.component.test.tsx` the tiers' `Product` structured data. `takumi.render.test.ts` renders cards in English, German, Ukrainian and Japanese and the logo, and checks their size.
- `e2e/specs/seo.spec.ts` checks that the sitemap index names the pages' sitemap and that every sitemap it names answers as XML, compares the pages' sitemap with the prerendered pages that allow indexing, requests every URL of every sitemap and expects `200` without a redirect, and opens the home page, a docs page and a blog post in English and Japanese to check their social tags and the image they name. It also checks that the prerendered home page carries the organisation's structured data and a `Product` with its price for each tier and that the logo is served at its size. `e2e/specs/content-seo.spec.ts` checks what the content adds: that the sitemap lists the docs in every language, that the `<lastmod>` of every published post in every locale is its `updated`, or its `date` when there is none, and that no other URL has one, that the blog index and posts name the feed of their locale, and that every feed lists exactly the published posts of its locale with their publication dates. `e2e/specs/course-seo.spec.ts` checks that the course sitemap lists the catalogue, the seeded course and its free lesson in every locale, each with a `<lastmod>`, and none of the lessons a visitor may not watch, and that the course page carries its `Course` structured data while the lessons outside the sitemap carry `noindex`.
- After a deploy, `verify-deployment.ts` requests <RepositoryFact name="liveSiteCheckPaths" />, the sitemap and the site card among them.
