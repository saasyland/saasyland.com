## The short answer

**Choose Clerk** if you want sign-in, user management and organizations working this afternoon, with prebuilt components, bot protection and SOC 2 and HIPAA reports handled by someone else, and you expect to stay under 50,000 monthly retained users for a while, or have the margin to pay per user beyond that.

**Choose Better Auth** if you want your users in your own database, no per-user fee at any scale, and features such as two-factor, passkeys, organizations and SSO as plugins you configure in code. It is what we run.

**Keep Auth.js** if you already have it and it works. For a new project, its own maintainers recommend Better Auth.

## The auth tax: what each option costs as you grow

### What Clerk charges, from its pricing page

Clerk prices by **monthly retained users** (MRU): "a user who visits your app in a given month at least one day after signing up". Someone who signs up and never comes back is not counted, which is generous, and it is not the same as the monthly active users other services count.

- **Hobby** is free, with "50,000 MRU limit per app", up to three dashboard seats and a fixed seven-day session lifetime. It does not include multi-factor authentication or passkeys.
- **Pro** costs $25 a month, or $20 billed annually, and includes "50,000 MRUs included per app". Beyond that, each retained user costs $0.02 a month from 50,001 to 100,000, $0.018 up to 1,000,000, $0.015 up to 10,000,000 and $0.012 above.
- **Business** costs $300 a month, or $250 billed annually, with the same per-user rates, ten dashboard seats and SOC 2 report access.

Clerk raised the free allowance on 5 February 2026: "50,000 Monthly Retained Users are now free in every application, up from 10,000". Comparisons that quote a free tier of 10,000 users describe the old plans.

### Clerk’s bill at scale

My arithmetic, from that table, for one application on Pro at monthly list price, before any add-on:

| Monthly retained users | Clerk Pro, per month | Better Auth, per month | Auth.js, per month |
| ---------------------- | -------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------ |
| 10,000                 | $25                  | $0 in licence fees     | $0 in licence fees |
| 50,000                 | $25                  | $0 in licence fees     | $0 in licence fees |
| 100,000                | $1,025               | $0 in licence fees     | $0 in licence fees |
| 1,000,000              | $17,225              | $0 in licence fees     | $0 in licence fees |

At 100,000 users the per-user part is 50,000 × $0.02. At 1,000,000 it is that $1,000 plus 900,000 × $0.018, $16,200, so about $206,700 a year. The Hobby plan would cover the first two rows for nothing, without multi-factor authentication or passkeys.

The add-ons are billed on top. Organizations are free up to 100 monthly retained organizations; past that, Clerk’s FAQ says "you will be required to upgrade to the B2B Authentication add-on", which costs $100 a month and $1 per further organization up to 1,000, falling to $0.60 above 100,000. Pro and Business include one enterprise SSO connection, then charge $75 a month for each of the next fourteen. SMS codes cost $0.01 each in the US and Canada and the market rate elsewhere. A B2B product with 500 retained organizations and ten customers on SAML would add $500 and $675 a month to the figures above.

That is the **auth tax**: a bill that grows with exactly the number you are trying to grow. It is also why a hosted service can give you so much for free at the start.

### What the libraries cost instead

**Better Auth** is MIT-licensed, and its comparison page sums up the case: "Users stay in your database, not a third-party service" and "No per-user costs". You pay for the database it writes to, the email and SMS provider that sends its codes and links, and the hosting it runs on, which your application needs anyway.

Better Auth also sells **Better Auth Infrastructure**, a managed dashboard, audit log and security service for a self-hosted Better Auth: a free Starter plan; Pro at $20 a month with 20,000 audit log events and 10,000 security detections included; self-service SSO and directory sync at one connection included and $50 a month per further connection; and email at $0.001 and SMS at $0.09 per message. Its pricing page is clear that "the Better Auth framework is free and open source", and none of the plugins in this article needs Infrastructure, except that letting customers configure their own SSO connection through an onboarding link is an Infrastructure feature.

**Auth.js** is ISC-licensed and free in the same way.

**What no table shows is your time.** With a library, you build the sign-in screens, send the emails, keep the dependency current and own the incident when something breaks. With Clerk, its prebuilt components and its operations team do that for you. If your team is small and your users are few, that can be worth more than the auth tax costs you.

## Who owns the user table

**With Clerk, Clerk holds it.** Your users, sessions and credentials live in Clerk’s service, and its security page says Clerk "does not offer regional data residency or region selection. Data is hosted on US infrastructure". Every plan includes "full data exports", and the export is where lock-in shows: according to Clerk’s documentation, "only the Clerk Dashboard export includes password digests and time-based one-time password (TOTP) secrets", the CLI and Backend API exports cannot include password digests, and "no export includes backup codes". Moving off Clerk is possible with the Dashboard export, and users who rely on backup codes will need new ones.

Clerk hashes passwords with bcrypt. Better Auth’s Clerk migration guide notes that Better Auth uses scrypt by default, so migrated users can sign in with their old passwords only once you configure Better Auth to verify bcrypt hashes.

**With Better Auth or Auth.js, you hold it.** The users, sessions, accounts and verification tables sit in your own database, through adapters for Drizzle, Prisma, Kysely, MongoDB and others, and you back them up, query them and join them to your own tables like any other data. The lock-in moves from a vendor to a library: you depend on its schema and its API, but you can read its source and fork it, and Better Auth publishes a migration guide from Auth.js.

## Self-hosting and where it runs

**Clerk** is a managed service, so there is nothing to host and nothing you can host. It has SDKs for the major frameworks, TanStack Start among them through `@clerk/tanstack-react-start`, and a public status page.

**Better Auth** runs inside your application, on any runtime your application runs on. It documents integrations for Next.js, TanStack Start, React Router, Nuxt, SvelteKit, SolidStart, Astro, Hono, Express and others, and its Cloudflare guide covers Workers: read the client IP from `CF-Connecting-IP`, use a database driver that queries over HTTP, and do not use Workers KV as `secondaryStorage`, because KV "cannot meet the `secondaryStorage` contract".

**Auth.js** runs inside your application too, with official adapters for most databases, Cloudflare D1 included.

## Organizations and multi-tenancy

If you sell to companies, the organization model matters more than the sign-in form.

**Clerk** includes organizations on every plan, free up to 100 monthly retained organizations of up to 20 members, with admin and member roles, custom permissions and invitations. An organization counts as retained "when it has at least 2 members and at least one of those members is a monthly retained user for that month". Custom roles, unlimited members and verified domains are part of the B2B add-on. Its `<OrganizationSwitcher />` component "handles all Organization-related flows", and enterprise SSO supports SAML, OIDC and EASIE.

**Better Auth** has an organization plugin with members, invitations and roles; teams, which "allow you to group members within an organization"; and dynamic access control, which "allows you to create roles at runtime for organizations". Its SSO plugin connects "each organization to its own OIDC or SAML 2.0 identity provider", and a separate SCIM plugin provisions users from a directory. All of it lives in your database, at no per-organization price.

**Auth.js** has no organizations. Its role-based access control guide shows how to add a role to the session yourself.

## Two-factor, passkeys and the rest of sign-in

| Feature             | Clerk                                                   | Better Auth                                           | Auth.js                                                    |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Two-factor          | Authenticator apps, SMS codes and backup codes, on Pro  | Two-factor plugin: TOTP, one-time codes, backup codes | Not built in                                               |
| Passkeys            | On Pro and Business                                     | Passkey plugin, `@better-auth/passkey`                | WebAuthn provider, experimental                            |
| Social sign-in      | Up to three providers on Hobby, unlimited on paid plans | Built-in social providers, plus generic OAuth         | More than 80 preconfigured providers                       |
| Email and password  | Passwords checked against leaked password databases     | Included, with verification and password reset        | Credentials provider; you write the storage and the checks |
| Magic links and OTP | Email links and six-digit email codes, on every plan    | Magic link and email OTP plugins                      | Email magic links                                          |
| Admin and bans      | Bans on Pro; five impersonations a month on every plan  | Admin plugin: roles, bans and impersonation           | Not built in                                               |
| Prebuilt UI         | `<SignIn />`, `<UserProfile />` and more                | None: you build the screens                           | A default sign-in page                                     |

Two of those rows changed this year. Clerk eliminated its Enhanced Authentication add-on in February 2026 and moved multi-factor authentication into Pro, so two-factor no longer needs an add-on there; it is still not on the free plan. And Better Auth moved several plugins into their own packages, passkeys, SSO and API keys among them, so older tutorials import them from the wrong place.

Auth.js is direct about its limits. Its WebAuthn page says "the WebAuthn / Passkeys provider is experimental and not recommended for production use", and its credentials page recommends "a more modern and secure authentication mechanism" and reminds you that you must "provide the necessary logic, eg. to encrypt passwords, add rate-limiting, add password reset functionality".

## Auth.js in 2026: maintained by the Better Auth team

On 22 September 2025, Better Auth announced that "Auth.js, formerly known as NextAuth.js, is now being maintained and overseen by Better Auth team". The announcement promised to "keep addressing security patches and urgent issues", and added: "we strongly recommend new projects to start with Better Auth unless there are some very specific feature gaps (most notably stateless session management without a database)". The Auth.js team’s own GitHub discussion says the same, and both of its websites now carry a banner saying the project is part of Better Auth.

That one exception has since closed: Better Auth’s session documentation says it "supports stateless session management without any database".

Auth.js v5 is still a beta. On 20 November 2026, the latest releases on npm were `next-auth` 4.24.15 and 5.0.0-beta.32, both published on 20 July 2026. An existing Auth.js app is not in danger, and there is no reason to rewrite one that works. A new one should start somewhere with a future.

## When Clerk is the better choice

Clerk is a good product, and for many teams it is the right one:

- **You want it working today.** `<SignIn />` "renders a UI to allow users to sign in or sign up", and `<UserProfile />` gives users "a beautiful, full-featured account management UI". With a library, those screens are yours to build, translate and test.
- **You need compliance paperwork.** Clerk says it "has been SOC 2 Type 2 and HIPAA certified since May 2022", with the SOC 2 report on Business and above and a HIPAA business associate agreement on Enterprise.
- **You want abuse handled for you.** Brute force prevention and basic bot protection are on every plan, with advanced protections on Business.
- **Your user count will stay modest.** Up to 50,000 monthly retained users per application, Clerk costs nothing on Hobby and $25 a month on Pro, and users who sign up once and leave are never counted.
- **You sell to enterprises soon.** SAML, OIDC and EASIE connections, directory sync and organizations work without building an admin for them.

Weigh that against two things that grow with success: the per-user and per-organization bill, and the cost of leaving once your users, sessions and second factors live in someone else’s service.

## Why saasyland.com runs Better Auth

saasyland.com sells one-time licences, runs on a Cloudflare Worker and speaks <RepositoryFact name="languages" /> languages. Its authentication, all of it public under the MIT licence, is Better Auth on D1:

- **Users, sessions and rate limits are rows in D1**, through the Drizzle adapter, so the admin console joins them to orders and licences like any other table, and an admin can revoke any user’s sessions from their profile.
- **Email and password with verification required**, passwords of <RepositoryFact name="passwordMinLength" /> to <RepositoryFact name="passwordMaxLength" /> characters, sessions revoked on a password reset, and GitHub and Google sign-in with account linking and encrypted OAuth tokens.
- **Two-factor authentication with backup codes**, through Better Auth’s `twoFactor` plugin.
- **Roles, bans and impersonation** through the `admin` plugin, with the permission statements in the code.
- **Polar’s Better Auth plugin** connects each user to their customer portal and receives Polar’s verified webhooks; the [payments comparison](/blog/polar-vs-stripe-vs-lemon-squeezy) explains why we chose Polar.

The [Authentication](/docs/features/authentication), [Two-factor authentication](/docs/features/two-factor) and [Sessions](/docs/features/sessions) pages document every part.

The costs are real here too. We built and translated every sign-in, sign-up, verification and two-factor screen ourselves, because Better Auth ships none. The site has no passkeys today, and it has no organizations: the [Organizations](/docs/features/organizations) page says the Better Auth organization plugin is not installed, and the configurator lists multi-tenancy as arriving. And when Better Auth publishes a release, keeping our sign-in correct is our job, not a vendor’s.

SaaSy Land’s CLI is in beta: today it activates your licence, and project generation arrives in a later release, with Better Auth or no auth as the [configurator](/#cli) offers.
