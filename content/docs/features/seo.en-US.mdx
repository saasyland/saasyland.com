Every page describes itself to search engines and link previews through its route's `head`. The build turns those descriptions into `sitemap.xml` and a social card per prerendered page, so both are static files in `dist/client` and nothing about them runs in the Worker.

## Page metadata

`buildPageHead` in `src/lib/seo.ts` builds the head of every page. Most routes call it through `pageHead(ROUTES.X)`; docs pages, blog posts and course pages call it directly. It emits:

- the title, with the locale's separator and `APP_NAME`, the description, the canonical URL and an `alternate` link with `hreflang` for every locale and for `x-default`;
- for a page of a sequence, `rel="prev"` and `rel="next"` links to its neighbours (`sequence`): each page of the blog's archive names its own address as canonical, never the first page's, and links the pages before and after it;
- Open Graph: `og:site_name`, `og:title` (the page title without the site name, as Open Graph expects), `og:description`, `og:type`, `og:url`, `og:locale` and, for a page that belongs to a section, `article:section`;
- for a blog post, `article:published_time` and `article:modified_time`, from the `date` and `updated` of its frontmatter, and, from its `author`, the `author` meta tag and `article:author` with the author's profile when the post names one;
- the social image: `og:image`, `og:image:type`, `og:image:width` and `og:image:height` (<RepositoryFact name="socialImageWidth" /> by <RepositoryFact name="socialImageHeight" /> pixels) and `og:image:alt`, with `twitter:card` set to `summary_large_image`, `twitter:title`, `twitter:description`, `twitter:image` and `twitter:image:alt`.

A route's head keeps one tag per name or property, so a list such as the `article:tag` of a blog post's tags is rendered by the post page itself, and React places those tags in the document head. For the same reason the root route adds `og:locale:alternate` for the other locales in the document itself. The root route also sets the `robots` directive: `ROBOTS.FULL_PREVIEWS` in a `production` build, which lets search engines show large image previews and full snippets, and `ROBOTS.NON_PRODUCTION` (`noindex, nofollow`) in every other build mode, which keeps preview and test deployments out of search results.

A page keeps itself out of search results with the `robots` meta tag `ROBOTS.NOINDEX` (`noindex`). Every not-found page does: `notFoundHead` in `src/lib/not-found.ts` gives it the localized title and description from `errors.not-found` and `noindex`, without a canonical link or social card.

Docs pages name the section their breadcrumb shows: `fumadocs.indexes.ts` reads it with Fumadocs' `getBreadcrumbItems` when it builds the docs index, so `/docs` and the first page of each section have none. Blog posts name the blog, with the title from `pages.blog`.

## Structured data

`src/lib/seo.ts` also writes the JSON-LD that describes the site to search engines. Every JSON-LD script is serialized with `<` escaped, so no value can close the `script` element.

- The home page describes the organisation with `ORGANIZATION_STRUCTURED_DATA_HTML`: an `Organization` with `APP_NAME`, `APP_URL`, `CONTACT_EMAIL`, its logo at `ORGANIZATION_LOGO.PATH` and, in `sameAs`, its profiles on other sites, `APP_GITHUB_OWNER_URL` and `APP_INSTAGRAM_URL`. `sameAs` names the GitHub organisation rather than the repository, because it lists profiles of the organisation itself. Google reads this from the home page, so other pages do not repeat it.
- A blog post adds a `BlogPosting`, a `BreadcrumbList` and, when its frontmatter has `faq` entries, an `FAQPage` (`buildBlogPostStructuredDataHtml`); see [Blog](/docs/features/blog). The `BlogPosting` gives the headline, the description, the cover as an `ImageObject` of the `BLOG_COVER` size, the dates, the language as `inLanguage`, the tags in that language as `keywords`, the author as a `Person` with the `url` and `image` the frontmatter gives, and the organisation, logo included, as `publisher`. The breadcrumb leads from `APP_NAME` through the blog to the post, with the URLs and the blog's title of the page's locale.
- The blog index adds a `Blog` with its name, description, language and publisher, listing every published post of its locale as a `BlogPosting` with its headline, URL and publication date, and the posts of its first page as an `ItemList` (`buildBlogStructuredDataHtml`).
- Each later page of the blog's archive, `/blog/page/<n>`, adds a `CollectionPage` that is part of the `Blog`, its posts as an `ItemList` numbered across the whole archive, and a `BreadcrumbList` from `APP_NAME` through the blog to the page (`buildBlogArchiveStructuredDataHtml`); see [Blog](/docs/features/blog#routes).

## Social cards

Each prerendered page has its own card at `/images/og/<canonical path>.png`, and the home page at `/images/og/index.png` (`socialImagePath`). The card shows the wordmark, the section in the accent colour when there is one, the page's `og:title` and the domain, over the grid and glow of the site's backgrounds. Its alt text is that text in the page's locale: the title, the section and `APP_NAME`, joined by the locale's title separator.

Pages that are not prerendered, <RepositoryFact name="unlistedRoutes" /> and every path below them, share `/images/og/site.png`, which shows the wordmark alone and has `APP_NAME` as its alt text. Each such area names its paths in its `src/data/<area>.routes.ts`, as `COURSES_UNLISTED_ROUTES` does, and `UNLISTED_ROUTES` in `src/data/unlisted.ts` joins them: the TanStack Start plugin in `src/data/tanstack.vite.ts` leaves them out of prerendering and `buildPageHead` gives them the site card. A course page with a cover is the one exception: `courseShareImage` (`src/modules/course/course.cover.ts`) gives it its cover as `og:image` and `twitter:image`; see [Course covers](/docs/features/courses#course-covers).

The cards are React components, `PageCard` and `SiteCard` in `src/presentation/social-card.tsx`. `LogoCard` in the same file draws the organisation's logo, the mark of `APP_LOGO_PATH` on the light background, as a square PNG at `ORGANIZATION_LOGO.PATH`, the image the structured data names as the logo. `createSocialCardRenderer` in `src/integrations/takumi/takumi.render.tsx` draws them with [Takumi](https://takumi.kane.tw/docs/) (`@takumi-rs/core`), a Rust renderer with native Node bindings that needs no browser:

- The colours are the light theme's tokens. The renderer reads `src/presentation/styles/globals.css`, so `var(--background)`, `var(--foreground)`, `var(--ring)` and the `field-grid` and `field-signal` classes are the site's own.
- The fonts are the files the site serves. `takumi.fonts.ts` reads every `@font-face` rule with a `url()` source from `fonts.css`, the Geist and Geist Mono subsets with their unicode ranges, and from `@fontsource-variable/noto-sans-jp`, because the site leaves Japanese to the system font. Takumi loads only the subsets a card's text uses. Each face is registered under `APP_NAME` followed by its family name: Takumi bundles its own Latin-only Geist, which would otherwise be chosen instead of the site's Cyrillic and Latin Extended subsets.
- The logo is `APP_LOGO_PATH` from `src/presentation/branding`, the path `Wordmark` draws.

To change the design, edit `social-card.tsx`, run `bun run build:test` and open the cards in `dist/client/images/og`.

## Sitemap and robots.txt

`public/robots.txt` allows every path and names `https://saasyland.com/sitemap.xml`; `robots.test.ts` fails when that line does not match `APP_URL` and `SITEMAP_PATH`.

`scripts/generate-seo-files.ts` runs at the end of every build script, after `optimize-prerendered-html.ts`. It reads the head of every prerendered page in `dist/client` with `readPageHead` from `src/lib/page-head.ts`, then:

1. Writes `dist/client/sitemap.xml` with every page whose `robots` directive allows indexing and whose path `robots.txt` does not disallow (`listedPages` and `buildSitemap` in `src/lib/sitemap.ts`, with the RFC 9309 matching of `src/lib/robots.ts`). Each `<url>` lists the page's own `alternate` links as `xhtml:link` entries, `x-default` included. The build fails when a page names an alternate that the sitemap does not list, or when a URL appears twice. `ROBOTS.NON_PRODUCTION` does not count as a page's own directive, so every build mode writes the same sitemap.
2. Writes the RSS feed of the blog in every locale from the heads of the blog index and the posts the sitemap lists (`buildBlogFeeds` in `src/lib/feed.ts`); see [Blog](/docs/features/blog#feed).
3. Renders the site card and every page's card into `dist/client/images/og`, and the organisation's logo, and fails when two pages name the same image.

It prints how many pages the sitemap lists, how many feeds and cards it wrote and how long it took. A blog post's `<url>` carries a `<lastmod>`: `readPageHead` reads the post's `article:modified_time`, and `buildSitemap` writes its day as `YYYY-MM-DD`, in every locale. No other page has one, because docs, landing and legal pages carry no reliable modification date, and the time of the build would claim changes that never happened. Course pages are not in it: they are read from D1 on each request, and the sitemap is a file written by the build.

The Worker does not change. Takumi and the script run only at build time, and `src/server.ts` serves `sitemap.xml` and the cards from static assets like any other file, with the cache policy `public/_headers` sets for `/images/*`.

## Tests

- `seo.test.ts`, `page-head.test.tsx`, `sitemap.test.ts`, `feed.test.ts` and `robots.test.ts` in `src/lib/__test__` cover the tags and the structured data, reading the tags back, the sitemap, the feeds and the robots rules. `takumi.render.test.ts` renders cards in English, German, Ukrainian and Japanese and the logo, and checks their size.
- `e2e/specs/seo.spec.ts` compares the served sitemap with the prerendered pages that allow indexing, checks that the `<lastmod>` of every published post in every locale is its `updated`, or its `date` when there is none, and that no other URL has one, requests every URL in it and expects `200` without a redirect, and opens the home page, a docs page and a blog post in English and Japanese to check their social tags and the image they name. It also checks that the prerendered home page carries the organisation's structured data, that the logo is served at its size, that the blog index and posts name the feed of their locale, and that every feed lists exactly the published posts of its locale with their publication dates.
- After a deploy, `verify-deployment.ts` requests <RepositoryFact name="liveSiteCheckPaths" />, the sitemap and the site card among them.
