## Content

Posts live in `content/blog/<slug>.<locale>.mdx`, one file per locale. The frontmatter schema in `src/integrations/fumadocs/fumadocs.schema.ts` requires `title`, `author` and `date`, and accepts `description`, `excerpt`, `faq`, `image`, `published` (default `true`), `tags` and `updated`. A tag is one of `BLOG_TAGS` in `src/data/blog.ts`, and the schema rejects any other value; `PostTags` and `PostMeta` show each tag by its label under `tags` in `pages.blog`, so tags read in the page's language.

The featured posts are not frontmatter: `FEATURED_BLOG_POSTS` in `src/data/blog.ts` names them by slug, in the order the blog index and the landing page show them, so one line of one file chooses them for every locale. A slug a locale does not publish is skipped, and `src/data/__test__/blog.test.ts` fails when a slug names no post.

- `description` is the post's search description: the `description` and `og:description` tags and the feed use it. `excerpt` is the longer teaser under the title and on the blog index, and stands in for a missing `description`.
- `image` is the cover, a WebP file in `public/images/blog` of the size `BLOG_COVER` in `src/data/blog.ts` declares. The post page gives the cover that `width` and `height`, and the structured data describes it at that size; `src/data/__test__/blog.test.ts` fails when a cover has another size.
- `author` names the author by an id of `BLOG_AUTHOR` in `src/data/blog.ts`, such as `piotr-borowiecki`, and the schema rejects any other value. A post carries the id alone: see [Authors](#authors).

## Dates

`date` is the day a post was first published, and it never changes. `updated` is the day the post's facts or substance last changed, such as a price, a feature that shipped or a comparison that no longer holds; it does not move for a typo, formatting or a fix to a translation. Both are written `YYYY-MM-DD`, and every locale of a post carries the same two, because they date the post, not its translation. The blog index keeps both for every post, and they drive every date that readers and search engines see:

- `PostMeta` shows the publication date and, when `updated` is later than `date`, the update date, each in its own `<time>` element with a `dateTime`, formatted with the `calendarDate` format for the page's locale. The labels are the ICU messages `post.published` and `post.updated` in `pages.blog`, which take the formatted date as `{date}`. Every list of posts shows one date, in a `<time>` element (`PostDate` in `post-facts.tsx`): the publication date, except in **Recently updated** and in the archive sorted by **Updated**, which show the update date under the `post.updated` label, because that is the date those lists are ordered by.
- The `BlogPosting` JSON-LD gives `date` as `datePublished`, and `updated`, or `date` when there is none, as `dateModified`.
- `buildPageHead` writes the same two as `article:published_time` and `article:modified_time`, and `sitemap-pages.xml` gives the post's URL in every locale its `article:modified_time` as `<lastmod>`; see [Search and social previews](/docs/features/seo).

The frontmatter schema refuses a date in any other format and an `updated` earlier than `date`, so `bun run dev` and the build stop on such a post, and `scripts/check-content.ts` fails when a translation's `date` or `updated` differs from the English post's.

## Figures from the code

A post prints the values the code defines instead of typing them: `<RepositoryFact>` for counts, the coverage threshold (`coverageThreshold`), React's major version (`reactMajor`), the illustrative hourly rate (`illustrativeHourlyRate`, with `unit="currency"`) and the hours the Core price buys (`corePriceInHours`, with `unit="hour"`), and `<TierPrice>`, `<ActivationLimit>` and `<HeadStartTable />` for prices, machine limits and the head start. The `title` and the `faq` questions and answers are ICU messages: `formatPostText` in `src/lib/post-text.ts` formats them with `REPOSITORY_FACTS` and each tier's `<tier>Price` and `<tier>Machines`. `formatPostText` also passes `CLI_GENERATION_VALUES` from `src/data/cli.ts`, so a sentence that changes when project generation ships is written once as `{generation, select, available {…} other {…}}`, and `post-faq.test.ts` renders it with both statuses. It also passes `billingGeneration` (`BILLING_GENERATION` in `src/data/marketing.ts`), `affiliateProgram` and `affiliateHoldDays` from `src/data/affiliates.ts`, and `refundWithdrawalDays` (`REFUND_TERMS.WITHDRAWAL_DAYS`), and the same test renders the selects on `billingGeneration` and `affiliateProgram` with both statuses. `toBlogPostSummary` formats the title, and `<PostFaq entries={frontmatter.faq} />` renders the FAQ, with every answer in the prerendered HTML even while its panel is closed; the post adds no `FAQPage`, for the reason [Search and social previews](/docs/features/seo#structured-data) gives. `post-faq.test.ts` formats every post's title, questions and answers in its locale.

## Source

Every build turns the posts into a blog index per locale (see [Fumadocs](/docs/integrations/fumadocs#content-indexes)): the published posts under `/blog`, newest first, with the same i18n settings as the docs, each with its frontmatter, its table of contents and its reading time, counted with `Intl.Segmenter` for that locale. `loadBlogIndex(locale)` imports a locale's index, a file under `/assets/`, and `toBlogPostSummary` in `fumadocs.blog.ts` formats a post's title and localizes its URL. The loaders below read it in the browser on a client navigation, so moving through the blog never reaches the Worker. The same file picks every list the blog shows from that one index:

- `relatedBlogPosts`: the other posts that share the most tags with a post, the newest first among equals, as many as `RELATED_POSTS` allows.
- `blogHighlights`: the featured posts in the order of `FEATURED_BLOG_POSTS`; the newest other posts, as many as `BLOG_HIGHLIGHTS.LATEST`; and, from the posts left, those whose `updated` is later than their `date`, the most recently updated first, as many as `BLOG_HIGHLIGHTS.UPDATED`. No post appears in two of these lists.
- `blogNotes`: the landing page's lead post, the first featured post or else the newest, and the newest others.
- `blogArchivePageCount`, `blogArchivePage`, `blogArchivePath` and `blogArchiveSequence`: the archive's pages of `BLOG_ARCHIVE.PAGE_SIZE` posts, newest first, their addresses and their neighbours.

## Routes

- The `/blog` layout (`_site._marketing.blog.tsx`) sits in the `_site` and `_marketing` layouts, which add the site header, `PageFrame`, `Footer` and `BackToTop` around it (see [Landing page](/docs/features/landing-page#navigation-and-back-to-top)). It loads `docs.css` for article typography. The header marks **Blog** as the current section on the index and on every post. Its loader preloads `fumadocs` and `pages.blog` and reads the blog's title, and its head names the feed of the page's locale in a `<link rel="alternate" type="application/rss+xml">` (`blogFeedLinks`), so feed readers and browsers find it from the index and from every post.
- `/blog` (`_site._marketing.blog.index.tsx`) links the feed of its locale under the lead, then shows, from `src/presentation/components/custom/blog`, the featured posts with their covers (`FeaturedPosts`: the first as a wide card, the others in two columns), the **Latest** and **Recently updated** lists (`RecentPosts`) and the archive of every post (`PostArchive`). It describes itself to search engines as a `Blog` with every post and the posts of its first page as an `ItemList` (`buildBlogStructuredDataHtml`), and names `/blog/page/2` as its next page when there is one. `PostFinderLayout` sets them beside the finder; see [The finder](#the-finder).
- `/blog/page/<n>` (`_site._marketing.blog.page.$page.tsx`) is page `<n>` of the archive, from 2 to `blogArchivePageCount`, with its own title, **All posts, page \<n>**, its own canonical URL, `rel="prev"` and `rel="next"` links to its neighbours, and `PostPagination` links to the newer and older pages. Its structured data is a `CollectionPage` that is part of the blog, its posts as an `ItemList` numbered across the archive, and a `BreadcrumbList` (`buildBlogArchiveStructuredDataHtml`). Any other number, `1` or a number with a leading zero answers 404. The finder sits beside its posts too.
- `/blog/$` (`_site._marketing.blog.$.tsx`) finds one post in the blog index (404 when it is missing or unpublished), preloads its compiled MDX with `blogContent`, and composes `PostMeta` and `PostShare` in the bar under the title, `PostToc` (or, on small screens, `InlineTOC` under the same title), and after the article a second `PostShare` in a row of its own, `PostAuthor`, `PostCta` and `PostRelated`, all from `src/presentation/components/custom/blog`. It adds JSON-LD from `buildBlogPostStructuredDataHtml` and article metadata from `buildPageHead`, with the blog's title from `pages.blog` as its `article:section`, an `article:tag` for each tag in the page's language and its own social card; see [Search and social previews](/docs/features/seo). The byline in `PostMeta` names the author, and the name in `PostAuthor` links the author's page. The post wraps its MDX in Fumadocs' `RootProvider`, with search and the theme switch off and the strings of `useFumadocsI18n`, so the copy buttons of code blocks and heading anchors and the FAQ's accordion items are labelled in the post's language.

The landing page's Notes section (`notes-section.tsx`) shows the post `blogNotes` leads with as a card with its cover, beside the newest other posts and a link to `/blog`. The landing page is prerendered, so these links are in its HTML.

## The finder

`PostFinder` in `post-finder.tsx` keeps the archive's controls in reach wherever the reader is on the page, and `PostFinderLayout` sets it beside the posts of `/blog` and of every archive page. On screens at least `lg` wide it is a side panel that sticks below the header: a React Aria `SearchField`, the active filters under it, a `ToggleGroup` that sorts by **Newest**, **Updated** or **Shortest read**, another that filters by one topic with the number of posts of each, the largest first, and an index of every post that matches, with their number. Everything above the index stays in place; only the index scrolls, under its heading and count, filling the rest of the window's height and fading at its edges. The topics show the `BLOG_FINDER_TOPICS` largest, the smallest of them giving way to the chosen topic when it is not among them, so the controls keep their height; **Show all \<n> topics** opens every topic in a popover that scrolls on its own and closes on the topic picked. The index keeps a minimum height of a few posts (`BLOG_FINDER_CLASSES.INDEX`): when the window is too short for the controls and that much of the index, the sort, the topics and the index scroll together under the search and the active filters. The index is `PostRailList`, the list of the rail beside a post, so the two pages read as one. There is no sort by popularity: the site counts no views.

On narrower screens the same element is a bar that sticks below the header from the top of the page: the search field and **Filters**, which says how many filters are active and opens a sheet with the sort, every topic, **Clear all** and **Show \<n> posts**. The sheet is the shadcn `Sheet`, a React Aria modal dialog: focus stays inside it, Escape closes it, and focus returns to **Filters**.

A chosen topic and a sort other than **Newest** stay in view as active filters under the search field, each with a button that removes it, and **Clear all** clears them with the search. Every change applies at once, and `useBlogView` in `src/hooks/use-blog-view.ts` scrolls the archive into view, below the header and the bar, when its heading is below the upper half of the window or scrolled past, so the reader sees the posts the change matched; while the heading is in view, the page stays where it is. The count under the archive's heading is the one live region that announces the matches. On an archive page the index narrows as the reader types, and **Enter**, a topic or a sort opens `/blog` at its archive with that view.

## The archive: search, topics, sorting and more posts

`PostArchive` lists the posts that match the finder, `BLOG_ARCHIVE.PAGE_SIZE` at a time, as rows of `PostRow`, under its heading and the number of posts or matches. Without a search or a topic it lists every post of the locale.

- **Search.** `filterBlogPosts` in `src/lib/blog-archive.ts` keeps the posts that contain every word of the query, in any order, in the title, the summary, a tag or its label in the page's language. `normalizeSearchText` compares without case, without Latin accents and with full-width letters folded, so `securite` finds _Sécurité_ and `ＴａｎＳｔａｃｋ` finds _TanStack_. The search runs in the browser over the index the page already has, as the reader types.
- **Sorting.** `sortBlogPosts` orders by the publication date, by the latest change (`updated`, or `date` for a post never updated) or by reading time, the newest first among equals.
- **The address is the state.** The route's `validateSearch` reads `q`, `tag`, `sort` and `page` with the helpers of `src/lib/search-params.ts`; `stripSearchParams` keeps the default sort out of the address. Every change replaces the history entry without scrolling, so **Back** leaves the blog instead of undoing a keystroke, and a link such as `/blog?tag=auth&sort=updated` opens the archive that way.
- **The prerendered page is the default view.** `/blog` is a static file, and Cloudflare serves the same file whatever the query string, so the server's HTML always lists the first page, newest first. `PostArchive` reads the address only once the page has hydrated (`useHydrated`), so the first render in the browser matches that HTML and React never discards it; the search, topic and sort then apply. `PostFinder` reads the address the same way, so the prerendered finder shows the default view, and its controls work once the page has hydrated.
- **More posts.** Under the rows, **Load more posts** is a link to `/blog/page/2`. Without JavaScript, and for crawlers, it opens that page, which links on to the next; with JavaScript, a plain click adds the next page of rows in place and records `page=2` in the address, and a click that opens a new tab or window still goes to the link. There is no infinite scroll: every post stays one link away.
- **Nothing found.** When no post matches, the archive says so and **Show all posts** clears the search and the topic. The finder's index then says **No matches**.

Each control is named in every locale under `archive` in `pages.blog`, and the topics by their labels under `tags`.

## Beside and after a post

On screens at least `xl` wide, a post has two rails: `PostRail` on the left lists every post of the locale, newest first, and marks the one being read with `aria-current="page"` (TanStack Router's own `data-status="active"`); `PostToc` on the right is the table of contents. Each rail sticks below the header and scrolls on its own when it is taller than the window, and `PostRail` scrolls the current post into its view once the page has hydrated. On narrower screens the rail is left out, and the post ends with `PostRelated`, its related posts and **See all \<n> posts**, which links to `/blog`. Its list is `PostRailList`, which the finder's index on `/blog` shares.

`PostToc` and the docs pages share one table of contents, `TableOfContents` (`custom/table-of-contents.tsx`). It marks the entry of the section being read with `aria-current="location"` and writes it in `--ring`, the teal of the header's progress line and of the current post in `PostRail`, and covers that entry's part of the rail with a teal line as thick as the progress line, as `PostRail` does for the current post. A section is being read once its heading has passed a reading line near the top of the window. While the next heading rises from the bottom of the window to that line, the line glides and resizes toward the next entry with the scroll and reaches it as that section becomes current; for a reader who prefers reduced motion it stays on the current entry and moves only when the section changes. When the current entry is outside the list's own view, the list scrolls to it without moving the page. `readingFrame` in `src/lib/table-of-contents.ts` computes the line and the current entry from positions the component measures when a `ResizeObserver` reports a change, so a scroll costs one `requestAnimationFrame` of arithmetic that sets two CSS variables and reads no layout. The prerendered HTML marks no entry, and the line appears once the page has hydrated.

## Sharing

`PostShare` renders the same list twice on every post: in the bar under the title, and in a row of its own after the article, above the author box. Every control shares the post's canonical URL in the page's locale, and every network receives the post's title.

- **Copy link** is a React Aria `Button`. It writes the URL to the clipboard, turns its icon into a check for a moment and shows a `toast.success` notification, which the notifications region announces to screen readers. When the browser refuses, `toast.error` says so.
- **X, LinkedIn, Bluesky, Hacker News and Reddit** are plain links to each network's own share page, opened in a new tab with `rel="noopener noreferrer"`. The page loads no script, widget or tracking pixel from any of them, sets no cookie for them and adds no tracking parameter to the URL, so the canonical URL is what gets shared and the links work without JavaScript.
- **Share by email** is a `mailto:` link with the title as the subject and the URL as the body, opened in the reader's mail app rather than a new tab.
- **More ways to share** opens the system share sheet with `navigator.share`, where the browser has one, as on phones. The button keeps its place from the first render and becomes visible once the page has hydrated in such a browser, so nothing moves. When the reader cancels the sheet nothing happens; when sharing fails the button copies the link instead.

The networks' marks are their official logos from Simple Icons, in `src/presentation/components/custom/icons.tsx`, drawn in the text colour; the other icons come from Lucide. Each control is named in every locale under `post.share` in `pages.blog`.

Every heading in a post links to itself, and its copy button copies the link to that section, so a reader can share one section as well as the whole post.

## Authors

Every author is described once, in `src/data/blog.ts`: `BLOG_AUTHOR` names the ids a post's `author` may take, and `BLOG_AUTHORS` gives each id the author's name, the photo, a WebP file in `public/images/blog/authors` of `AUTHOR_IMAGE_SIZE` pixels square, and the profiles on other sites, each a network of `AUTHOR_PROFILE` and a URL. The role and the biography are text in the page's language, so they are messages under `authors.<id>` in `pages.blog`. The byline, the author box, the author page, the `author` meta tags and the structured data all read these two sources, and `src/data/__test__/blog.test.ts` fails when a photo is missing or has another size.

- **The author box.** `PostAuthor` closes every post in a card of its own: the photo, **Written by**, the name as a link to the author's page with `rel="author"`, the role, the biography and `AuthorLinks`, the author's profiles as icon links named in the page's language (**Piotr J. Borowiecki on GitHub**) that open in a new tab with `rel="me noopener noreferrer"`.
- **The author page.** `/authors/<id>` (`_site._marketing.authors.$author.tsx`) shows the photo, the name as the page's heading, the role, the biography and `AuthorLinks`, then every post by the author in the page's language as rows of `PostRow`, with their number, and closes with **Views are my own.** (`author.disclaimer`). An id that `BLOG_AUTHOR` does not name answers 404. Its title is the author's name and its description the biography, with a canonical URL, `hreflang` alternates, `og:type` set to `profile` and its own social card; the prerenderer reaches it through the byline of every post, in every locale, so the sitemap lists it like any other page. Its structured data is a `ProfilePage` whose `mainEntity` is the author (`buildAuthorStructuredDataHtml`); see [Search and social previews](/docs/features/seo#structured-data).
- **Adding an author.** Add an id to `BLOG_AUTHOR`, the author's entry to `BLOG_AUTHORS` and the photo, then the role and the biography under `authors.<id>` in `pages.blog` of every locale; the author page's loader parses that record for every id, so a locale without it fails the build.

## Related posts

`PostRelated` lists the posts `relatedBlogPosts` picked, with their dates and reading times, so every post links to others on the same subjects, and ends with a link to every post. It renders nothing when the locale has no other post.

## Feed

Each locale has an RSS 2.0 feed of its blog at `ROUTES.BLOG.FEED`, localized like a page: `/blog/rss.xml`, `/de-DE/blog/rss.xml` and so on. `scripts/generate-seo-files.ts` writes the feeds after the build with `buildBlogFeeds`, from the heads of the prerendered pages, so they cost the Worker nothing and say what the pages say:

- The channel takes its title, link and description from the locale's blog index, and names its own URL in an `atom:link rel="self"`.
- Each post is an item with its title, canonical URL as `link` and `guid`, `article:published_time` as `pubDate`, the author as `dc:creator`, each tag in the page's language as a `category` and its description. Items are newest first, and `lastBuildDate` is the latest publication or update date of the posts.
- Only pages the sitemap lists are read, and an unpublished post is never prerendered, so a draft never reaches a feed. The archive's later pages, under `/blog/page/`, are not posts and are left out. The build fails when a post has no publication date or a locale has no blog index.

The prerenderer follows links, and the feed link on the blog index points at a file the build writes afterwards, so `shouldPrerender` in `src/lib/prerender.ts` skips every path with a file extension. After a deploy, `verify-deployment.ts` requests the English feed with the rest of the live checks.

## Tags

Tags label posts, filter the archive on `/blog` and fill the `article:tag` tags, the feed's categories and the structured data's `keywords`, but they have no pages of their own: the topic filter lives in the `tag` query parameter, which the prerendered `/blog` names as its own canonical URL. Most tags label very few posts, so a page per tag in every locale would list what the blog index already lists, and search engines treat such near-empty archives as thin content. Tag pages are worth adding once each tag gathers enough posts to need its own list; until then they would only multiply the URLs to crawl.

## Editing

Posts are MDX files in Git. The admin has no blog editor: a post is written, reviewed and published as a pull request.

## Checks

Run `bun run check:content`, which `bun run check` also runs, after adding a post. It verifies that every locale exists, that titles, descriptions and excerpts are translated, that code blocks are identical, and that every locale of a post has the same `date` and `updated`. In French posts it also fails an ordinary space where French typography needs a no-break space (see [No-break spaces](/docs/conventions/translations#no-break-spaces)).

`blog-routes.component.test.tsx` checks the featured, latest and recently updated posts, the search, topic filter, sorting and **Load more posts** of the archive, the archive pages, the rail beside a post, every share target's URL, the copy confirmation, the system share sheet, the author box, the author page and its structured data, the related posts, the feed link and the post's metadata; `blog-archive.test.ts` checks the search and the sorting, `fumadocs.blog.test.ts` the lists picked from the index and `feed.test.ts` the feeds. In the browser suite, `blog-index.spec.ts` searches, filters, sorts and loads more posts with JavaScript, follows the archive's pages without it, checks the canonical and neighbouring links of every archive page and the landing page's Notes; `blog-share.spec.ts` shares a post on a desktop and on a phone, with and without JavaScript, `blog-author.spec.ts` follows the byline to the author page in English and Polish, reads the `Person` of a post and the `ProfilePage` of the author page, and fails when a rendered page or a message names the author’s employer, and `content-seo.spec.ts` reads every locale's feed and compares it with the published posts. `blog-finder.component.test.tsx` checks the finder: its index, the topics it shows and hides, the active filters, the sheet, scrolling to the posts and the archive pages. In the browser, `blog-index.spec.ts` also keeps the finder in view while the page scrolls, filters from it on a desktop and from its sheet on a phone, and searches the whole blog from an archive page.
